When you have time

For you, Naoise 🌿

This is for the moments when anxiety isn't hitting you right now, but you want to actually work through what's been sitting with you.

↓ scroll
Anxiety is just a fear you haven't finished thinking through yet.
This is a step-by-step process for when you have the space to actually sit with what's bothering you. Not to suppress it — to walk through it all the way to the end, where you'll always find solid ground.
1
Name it precisely
Not "I'm anxious." What exactly are you afraid of? One sentence.
Anxiety hides in vagueness. Make yourself write down the actual fear — who does what, what happens. Specific fear is a fear you can actually work with. Vague fear just spins.
For example
"I'm afraid I'll fail this and never find something I'm proud of."
If you can't name it in one sentence, keep going until you can.
2
Is this real or imagined?
Has this actually happened before?
If yes — you survived it, which means you have evidence. If no — what is the actual basis for the fear? Anxiety invents threats. Check if there's real ground under it or just a story you're telling yourself.
For example
"I've been in tough spots before. I didn't always know how it would work out. But I'm still here."
Evidence is more reliable than imagination.
3
Check the evidence
What does the actual pattern in your life say?
Not what anxiety says — the facts. Look at your history. What does it actually show about how things tend to go for you? Your track record is probably better than your fear wants you to believe.
For example
"Every time I've been in a hard place, I've found a way through. The evidence says I figure it out."
Your history is data. Use it.
4
Finish the thought
Anxiety stops at 70. You need to go to 100.
Say the thing happens — and then what? And then what? And then what? Keep pulling the thread until you land somewhere solid. The fear of finishing the thought is always worse than the thought itself. You always land somewhere liveable.
For example
"It goes wrong → I feel awful for a bit → I try something else → something else comes up → life continues."
The spiral stops when you choose to finish it.
5
Is this actually the end?
If the worst happened — is it genuinely over?
Would you find a way through? Would life continue? Almost always yes. That's not dismissing the pain — that's being honest about your own resilience. You are more capable of surviving hard things than anxiety wants you to believe.
For example
"Even if this takes much longer than I planned, I'm not finished. I'm uncomfortable, not done."
You've survived 100% of your hardest days so far.
6
Zoom out
Will this matter in one year? Three years?
If no, the fear is real but the size you're giving it isn't. Right-sizing isn't dismissing the fear — it's giving it an accurate amount of your energy. Not all of it. Just what it actually deserves.
For example
"In three years I'll be somewhere new. This specific worry won't even be a memory I carry."
Most things that feel permanent are temporary with a long shadow.
7
Act or release
One thing you can do today? Do it. If not — put it down.
If there is something — even one small thing — do it. Action is the only real antidote to anxiety. Even the smallest step changes your relationship to the fear. If there's nothing you can do right now, you have full permission to set it down. Not forever. Just for today.
For example
"I can't control how this plays out. But I can do one small thing today. So I'll do that and close the tab."
You don't have to solve it. You just have to move, or rest.

You walked through it,
Naoise. That's the whole practice.

You named it, finished the thought, right-sized it, and found your footing. That's not nothing. That's everything. Come back to this as many times as you need.
